# Nivel design system

The brand reads "Nivel" in the header next to the Nivel mark, and "Nivel by The Equal Lens" in the install manifest. Brand files (the mark in colour, black and white, the app tiles, and the Lensy illustrations) are in `Nivel/` at the repo root; the app uses copies in `src/assets/nivel-mark.svg`, `public/favicon.svg`, `public/icons/` and `public/illustrations/`.

**The logo (from 3 October 2026):** the source is The Equal Lens's `Solutions/Nivel - Logo.svg` (1254 by 1254, transparent; white- and black-ground versions beside it): an N of four nodes with a dashed teal parity line. Its dark outline (`#200E2B`) blends into dark grounds while the lilac, violet and teal fills stay legible, so one transparent file serves both themes. Every use is derived from it:
- **Header and footer mark** (`src/assets/nivel-mark.svg`): the SVG cropped to the artwork with an 8-unit margin (viewBox `209 301 834 619`), 32px tall in the header (28px on phones) and 24px in the footer.
- **Browser icon:** `public/favicon.svg` (a square crop, viewBox `196 180 860 860`), with `public/icons/favicon-32.png` and a 16, 32 and 48px `public/favicon.ico` for browsers without SVG icons. The boot screen shows the same file.
- **App icons:** `icon-192` and `icon-512` (transparent, the logo 86% of the width); `maskable-192` and `maskable-512` (white, 62%, inside the 80% safe circle); `apple-touch-icon` (white, 180px, 74%). The install prompt uses `icon-192`.
- Regenerate them from the source logo rather than editing the copies.

Source: The Equal Lens brand package (`equal-lens` skill, `references/brand.md` and `voice.md`). Patterns were checked against references from Lazyweb: investing.com's major-indices table with sparklines, WSJ's leaders and laggards tables, and ESG Book's score dashboard. We copied the first two and rejected the third's circular gauges.

## 1. Register
**Minimal register.** The data is the hero.
- Warm paper background.
- The Nivel mark in the header corner: two figures forming an N, violet `#9F7DE0` and lime `#D1F246`. The same colour mark works on light and dark.
- Hairline rules.
- Violet only in headings and the primary series.
- Lensy appears only in empty and not-yet states, as the supplied illustrations, never as decoration. Baloo 2 is not loaded.

### Dark theme (PARITY-10)
**Switching:**
- The theme follows the device (`prefers-color-scheme`) unless the header theme menu (System, Light, Dark, with a check on the current choice) sets `data-theme="light"` or `"dark"` on `<html>`.
- The choice persists in `localStorage` (`pt-theme`).
- A pre-paint script in `index.html` applies it before first render, so there's no flash.
- `?theme=dark` or `?theme=light` in the URL forces a theme, for screenshots and shared links.
- Dark mode uses the reversed (white) logo.

## 3. Type
- **Poppins** (400/500/600/700): headings, numbers, UI labels, table headers.
- **Nunito Sans** (400/600/700): body copy and table cells.
- Hero numbers use Poppins 600 with proportional figures. Tables use tabular figures.
- Headings are sentence case. Two-tone headlines use ink for the first half and `.accent` (violet) for the second.
- Headings use Poppins 600, never 700, with −0.01em tracking: h1 26–36px, h2 20px. This matches the restrained weights of investing apps (audit 2026-10).
- **12px floor everywhere (PARITY-28):** no text is smaller than 12px on any screen. That covers chart axes and reference labels, badges, scale ticks, tooltips, the quadrant labels, formula labels and monogram letters. Phones had the floor first (PARITY-24); desktop followed.

## 4. Layout
- Max content width 1120px, 16px side gutter on phones, 24px from 720px up.
- Cards: white, 1px `--line` border, 12px radius, no shadow.
- Sections are separated by a 1px `--mist` rule and 40px spacing.
- **Header (PARITY-17):** white and sticky, 64px. Left: the Nivel mark (32px tall, 28px on phones) and a two-line brand, "Nivel" (Poppins 600, 19px, line height 1.1) over "by The Equal Lens" (12px, line height 1.25, `--soft`), 1px apart so the two lines read as one lockup. Then the main nav (Indexes, Companies, Compare, Method) as underline tabs. Right: global search (38px field, at most 340px, with a ⌘K hint) and the theme menu: a borderless 40px icon button (44px on phones) that opens a small right-aligned menu. No install button. Below 900px the header is 56px: brand on the left, a search icon and the theme toggle on the right, and the nav moves to the bottom tab bar.
- **Bottom tab bar (below 900px):** four items with 22px line icons and 12px labels: Indexes (chart), Companies (buildings), Compare (balance scale), Method (info). Fixed, white, hairline top border, padded for the home indicator (`safe-area-inset-bottom`). The active item has a Material-style indicator pill (`--mist`, 62 by 30px, behind the icon), a bold violet label and `aria-current`, so the state never rests on colour alone.
- **Search sheet (below 900px):** the search icon opens a full-screen sheet: a 44px field (16px text, so iOS does not zoom) with a Cancel link, then up to 20 results as tall rows with logo or monogram. The page behind stops scrolling while it is open. The sheet renders in a portal on `<body>` so nothing (the tab bar included) can paint over it. No matches repeats the query and suggests a ticker, here and in the desktop dropdown.
- **Page head:** breadcrumb, title (with a 52px logo tile on company pages), a meta line of index names ("Nifty 50 · BSE Sensex", plain links separated by middots, not chips, no "In"), then section tabs. The first section after the page head has no top rule.
- **Title row (company pages):** the 52px logo tile, then the name (h1, line height 1.15) with the exchange and industry line 4px below it.
- **Index strip:** a white bar under the header on every page, scrolling horizontally. Each item shows name, headline value and like-for-like change. The current index is highlighted with a tint, and the strip scrolls itself (never the page) to bring it into view. The right edge fades on every width to show the strip scrolls, with 20px of end padding so the last index can scroll clear of the fade. On phones the chips are 34px tall with 6px of strip above and below, so the selected chip never touches the borders, and an invisible area makes each one 44px to tap. The fade is 24px, so the next index peeks in.
- **Offline bar:** when the device is offline, a slim tint bar under the header says "Offline. Showing data collected 1 October 2026." (`role="status"`).
- **URLs (PARITY-25):** pages have plain paths (`/company/TCS/calculations`, `/index/nifty50/companies`). Dots in tickers become underscores (`/company/EU-ADS_DE`). Links shared before October 2026 (`#/company/TCS`) redirect. The page function gives every page its own title, description, Open Graph tags and canonical URL from the live release, so every shared link previews its own page. See [decisions/0003](decisions/0003-path-urls-and-prerendered-pages.md) and [decisions/0014](decisions/0014-data-separate-from-app.md).
- **Footer:** the Nivel mark, one line ("By The Equal Lens, from companies' own gender pay filings, collected 1 October 2026. Not investment advice."), then Report a correction and Install the app. The trademark note lives on Method.
- **Corrections:** "Report a correction" opens an email to priyanshu.jain@theequallens.com, from the footer, the Method page's Corrections section and every company's Sources tab. The subject names the company.
- **Page change:** a 180ms opacity fade on the new page's content, off under reduced motion. A new page starts at the top; Back and Forward return to where the reader was.
- **Section tab change (PARITY-82):** only the section below the tabs changes. The page head (crumbs, title, logo, tabs) stays mounted and still, the page keeps its scroll so the tabs stay under the reader's finger, the new section fades in, and focus stays on the chosen tab. The pattern of native finance apps (Robinhood, Fidelity, Koyfin on Lazyweb), where a stock's header holds still while its sections switch.
- **Loading (PARITY-28):** until the code runs, the page shows the Nivel mark (48px, a slow pulse that is off under reduced motion) over "Loading Nivel" in `--soft`. If the code fails to load, the same spot says "Nivel could not load" with a reload link. See [decisions/0004](decisions/0004-data-as-separate-files.md).
- **Progressive loading (PARITY-79):** after the code, each page shows as soon as its own data is in; parts that need more data hold skeletons until it lands. See [decisions/0012](decisions/0012-progressive-loading.md).
  - **Skeletons:** the content's own shape in `--tint` (12px bars with 6px radius, 26px logo squares, 30px strip chips, table rows on a white card with hairline dividers), with a slow `--tint` to `--mist` sheen that is off under reduced motion. They fade in only after 300ms, so a fast or cached load never flashes grey. Each carries `role="status"` and a label ("Loading peers").
  - **Shapes in use:** a whole page (crumbs, title, three tiles, a list), the index strip (six chips), table rows (pace of change, movers, an index's companies, peers), and a single line (a scale card's market ranking).
  - **A part that failed** says "This part didn't load." (or "This part loads when you're back online.") in 13px `--soft`, with a quiet Try again button. It also retries by itself when the connection comes back. The rest of the page stays usable.
  - **Search** shows indexes at once and "Loading companies…" until companies arrive, never a false "no match".
- **Skip link and focus:** "Skip to content" is the first focusable element (violet pill, shown on focus). After every route change the page title updates ("TCS · Calculations · Nivel") and a polite live region announces it. On a new page, focus moves to the h1 without scrolling; on a section tab change it stays on the tab.
- **Card grids** use `auto-fill`, so a market with one index keeps a normal-width card.

## 5. Components
- **Stat tile:** label (Poppins 500, 13px, `--soft`), value (Poppins 600, 32–44px, `--ink`), one line of context. Never a gauge or ring.
- **Change label (PARITY-25):** a filled triangle plus signed percentage points (pp) in coloured text, 13px Poppins 600, with no fill, so it never looks tappable: `--good` for rises, `--bad` for falls. The triangle and sign mean it never relies on colour alone. Flat (|change| < 0.05 pp) shows "no change" in `--soft`; missing shows "n/a" in `--muted`.
- **Data badge:** `Reported`, `Rescaled`, `Estimated` (sun-deep text on tint), `Company report` (sun-deep, like Estimated), `Basis changed`, `Missing`. Every number that is not cleanly reported carries one.
- **Company-report figures (PARITY-92, [decision 0015](decisions/0015-coverage-accounted-for.md)):** where the statutory source has nothing, a figure can come from the company's own report. It always says so, in the same places a statutory figure names its source, with no new visual style:
  - the `Company report` badge, whose tooltip is the year's note ("The company's own group-wide figures (…), not a UK gender pay gap filing");
  - the Basis column of the years table ("Whole group, worldwide (company report)");
  - Calculations: "The company's own Annual Report 2025, page 64 (the whole group, worldwide)" as the source line, with the report's title and page as a link, and inputs labelled for what they are ("Mean pay gap, whole group", "Women in the group's workforce");
  - Sources: one sentence saying no local employer files, so these are the company's own figures and not the statutory ones, with the document linked. Japan names the document per figure ("Women in regular staff: 31.0%, from the company's ESG Data Book 2025, page 12 (the listed company's regular staff)").
  - India's annual-report filings list as "BRSR, in the company's annual report: PDF", with no empty XBRL link.
  - Company-report years stay out of UK index headlines and like-for-like change, which keep the statutory basis; Australian headlines are payroll-weighted from WGEA data and do not include them either.
- **Download dates come from the data:** Japan's source line gives the day the MHLW open data was downloaded, from the record, not fixed text.
- **Japan counts officers, not boards (PARITY-96):** the labour ministry field counts directors, auditors and executive officers. Japan pages say "Women among officers", with that list as the sub-line, "Women officers" as a column and "Officers: x of y" on a company. India's board seats keep "Women on board".
- **Status chip** (coverage map): `Live`, `Planned`, `Limited data`, with text labels, not colour alone.
- **Tables (data tables, `table.data`):**
  - 8px by 12px cells, tabular figures, `--tint` header row with 12px `--soft` labels; the sorted column's label turns `--ink`
  - sortable headers carry a two-triangle sort icon and `aria-sort`; the active direction is solid, the other faded
  - a bar above the table: row count on the left, a quiet "Download CSV" button on the right (exports the visible rows in the current sort)
  - optional rank column (`#`, muted, right-aligned) and a highlighted current row (tint fill plus a 3px violet inner left edge), used for peers
  - company cells: logo tile, name link, then ticker and market or industry as a muted sub-line
  - tables flow with the page: no inner scroll box. The header row stays pinned under the site header (`top: 64px`) while the page scrolls, with rounded outer corners on the first and last cells. Long headers wrap.
  - columns carry a priority: priority 2 hides below 1100px wide and priority 3 below 1000px (sparklines, sector, board), so a desktop table never scrolls sideways
  - **below 900px, lists become two-line rows** (PARITY-23, after testing on a Pixel 9). A header row inside the list (tint) holds the count and one dropdown that both chooses the figure and sorts by it, plus a direction button. Each row: the logo, then the name (Poppins 600, 15px, one line, ellipsis) over a muted subtitle that says only what the list does not already say (the sector; the market too when the list mixes markets). On the right, the figure for the chosen column (16px, tabular), so the visible numbers always run in sorted order and never need a per-row label. When the figure is share of pay, a small second line gives the 1-year change in coloured text. Sorting by name shows share of pay and says so in the header.
  - **row rules:** figures stay right-aligned in one column, so no pills beside them. An estimated figure gets a "≈" prefix with the reason in its tooltip; rescaled and basis-change flags live on the company page. When a second-line value is missing, the line is left out and the figure centres in the row, never a column of "n/a". Everything else lives on the company page or behind the Sort menu.
  - small reference tables (years, a sector breakdown, the markets list) keep detail cards instead: details in a two-column grid under a hairline, long text across both columns.
  - list bars carry a short count ("50 companies") with Sort and a direction button; explanations sit in the text above the table, and there is no CSV button on phones.
  - the Sort dropdown truncates its label so the direction button always stays on screen. Small tables (years, sectors, markets) drop the bar entirely.
  - every table goes through this component, including chart frames' table views and the markets table, so no table on a phone scrolls sideways
  - the whole row opens the company, with the name link kept for keyboard and screen-reader users
  - **list state lives in the URL** (PARITY-24): a page's main list keeps its sort (`sort=-e`), its index filter (`index=all`), its search (`q=`) and Show all (`all=1`) in the URL's query string (`/companies?index=all&sort=-e`), written with `replaceState` so it adds no history entries. Back returns to the same list, in the same order, at the same scroll position, and the URL can be shared. Compare's filters and the map's Chart or Table choice work the same way.
  - **Show all:** on phones a long list shows its first 20 (index pages) or 30 (Companies, parity map) rows, then a full-width "Show all 225" row button.
  - **empty state:** a filtered list with no rows says what was searched, suggests a ticker or sector, and offers Clear search (and Search all indexes when one index is selected). Never "0 companies" over an empty card.
  - Home's pace of change is a small table (market, median change, companies compared, rose / fell, years to parity), shown as detail cards on phones.
  - a one-line key sits under long lists: "≈ means estimated or derived rather than reported. pp means percentage points.
- **Meter (inline data bar):** the value, then a 52px by 6px `--line` track with a `--s1` fill and a 2px ink tick at parity. Employment parity and share of pay run 0–100% (tick at 50%); wage parity runs 0–140% (tick at 100%). Replaces bare percentages wherever a value has a parity point.
- **Tabs:** underline tabs for sections that have their own URL (company pages: Overview, Trend, Peers, Calculations, Sources; index pages: Overview, Companies, Sectors; Compare: Countries, Sectors, Parity map). The first tab is the page's base URL. 14px Poppins 500, `--soft`, active `--violet` with a 2px underline and `aria-current="page"`. They scroll sideways on phones, where each tab is 44px tall, and the row scrolls itself to keep the current tab in view. Switching tabs follows the section tab change rule in Layout.
- **Feedback and motion (PARITY-82):**
  - **Hover only where a pointer can hover** (`@media (hover: hover)`), so a tap on a phone never leaves a control looking hovered.
  - **Pressed:** pills, segmented options, buttons, dropdown and icon buttons shrink to 96% while pressed; strip chips, menu and search options, link cards and Show all take `--tint`; tabs and tab bar items darken to `--ink`. List rows already tint while pressed.
  - **Easing:** colour, background, border and shadow changes on controls take 150ms (presses 100ms), so a selection glides rather than snaps. A theme change switches the whole page in one frame, with no easing.

## 7. Voice (house rules)
- No em dashes. Use colons, commas and full stops. En dashes only for ranges.
- **Links in running text are underlined** (paragraphs, prose lists, meta lines and breadcrumbs), so they never rely on colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). Standalone links (`.go`), tabs, navigation and table name links are not.
- Bold leads a point. Numbers come with the finding attached.
- State limitations once, in one line, where they matter. Show the unflattering rows: missing companies, estimated values, broken filings.
- **UX copy (PARITY-65, the owner's call: "crisp, clear and concise text only"):**
  - **Lead with the finding.** The number or fact comes first; context follows.
  - **One idea per sentence.** Aim for 20 words or fewer.
  - **Don't repeat the UI.** Cut any sentence that restates a heading, a label, a chart or another sentence.
  - **Labels over sentences.** If a label and a number say it, add nothing.
  - **No hedging.** Drop "about", "roughly", "may", "can", "likely", "note that", "in other words" and "read these as". If a figure is uncertain, the badge or one plain reason says so.
  - **One caveat per section.** The detail lives on Method; link to it instead of explaining inline.
  - **Names are tags.** Write "Nifty 50", not "in the Nifty 50"; "FY26", not "for fiscal 2025-26".
  - **Headings say what you learn,** in six words or fewer.
  - **Links name the destination or action.** For example, "Parity map", "Download CSV".
  - **Sources:** Material Design's writing guidelines, Nielsen Norman Group's research on how people scan, and the GOV.UK style guide.
- This is not an ESG rating and not investment advice. Say so on the method page.
- **One name per measure (PARITY-24):** "Women's share of pay" (short "Share of pay"), "Employment parity" and "Wage parity", from `MEASURE` in `src/lib/compare.ts`. Each market's basis goes in brackets or a subtitle ("Employment parity (permanent staff)"), never in a new name. Figures a company reports are labelled in the filing's own words ("Women in regular staff"), because they are inputs, not our measures.

## 8. Internal tools: the Parity dashboard (PARITY-126)

The local dashboard for Parity, Nivel's company dataset (`pipeline/india/brsr1000/dashboard/`), uses the tokens, type and voice above. **Component library (owner, 12 October 2026).** Every component is one function in `ui.js` with its style in `ui.css`. The app (`app.js`) builds every view from them and adds no styles of its own. The page's Components view (`#view=library`) shows each component in each state.

- **Icons:** Material Symbols Rounded, from Google Fonts. They are 20px and outlined: 16px in tags and heads, 14px in badges and chips, and 40px light in empty states. An icon is decorative unless it carries the only label, in which case it gets `aria-label`. Icons stay hidden until their font has loaded, so a ligature name never shows. The Components page lists each icon's meaning.
- **Tooltips carry meta text and descriptions:** section descriptions, footnotes, how a figure was worked out, full file names and URLs, full log lines and reasons.
  - **The trigger:** an (i) button, or the element itself.
  - **The tooltip:** one floating element, ink on card, 13px, at most 300px wide.
  - **When it shows:** on hover on pointer devices, on keyboard focus, and on tap for (i) buttons on touch devices.
  - **What stays visible:** the primary line, so nothing a decision needs is hover-only on a phone.
- **Spacing scale:** 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 and 40px (`--sp-1` to `--sp-7`). Card rows pad 12px by 16px, sections are 40px apart (32px on phones), and groups inside a section start 24px down.
- **Type scale:** 12px (meta), 13px (secondary), 14px (body and rows), 16px (bar figures and pane titles), 20px (section heads), 24px (figures and the Components page title). The brand keeps section 4's 19px. The pane title is the pane's h2.
- **Radii:** 12px for cards, 8px for tiles, fields and buttons, 6px for badges, and a full radius for tags and chips.
- **One meaning per component:**
  - a **badge** labels status or data quality and is never interactive;
  - a **tag** is a filter that toggles, with a count;
  - a **chip** opens an outside page;
  - a **quiet button** is a secondary action.
- **List item:** a lead icon, a title (Poppins 600, 14px, ink), a meta line (13px soft, parts joined by middots), then an optional body and foot. A row that opens something carries a trailing chevron. A row that holds its own links opens on a click of its background.
- **Run row:** two lines (20px and 18px) in a 64px row, on the list-item grid: a 40px time column, the outcome icon in a 24px column aligned to line 1 with a 12px gap, the text, the duration, and a 20px chevron that ends at the padding edge. The chevron sits on line 1, with the duration.
