# Solve criteria check: Economic Prosperity and Learning

This checks the draft as the team reframed it on 2 October 2026 ([mit-solve-application.md](../plans/mit-solve-application.md)):

- **The problem:** unawareness of bias.
- **The users:** savers, investors and the stakeholders who influence employment decisions.
- **The outcome:** awareness, measured before and after.
- **The organisation:** a nonprofit with no payer and no partner for this work.

**The scale is ours.** Solve publishes no scoring scale, so the scores are an internal 1 to 5. Both challenge pages were read on 2 October 2026.

## 1. The verdict
- **Enter Economic Prosperity.** The draft scores about 3 of 5 across its screening criteria there, and under 2 under Learning.
- **Learning is a poor fit for Nivel.**
  - It asks how to "close global education gaps while improving outcomes for every learner".
  - Its focus areas are teachers, low-resource and emergency settings, assistive or multilingual tools, and assessment.
  - Nivel's learners are adult savers and professionals, not learners in an education system.
- **Solve lets one solution enter more than one Global Challenge,** but a team that advances in several must choose one. A second, weaker entry costs effort and gains little.
- **If The Equal Lens wants a Learning entry,** its school programmes for ages 5 to 18 fit that challenge far better than Nivel. That would be a separate solution and application, under the organisation's child-safety rules.
- **The weakest criteria under Economic Prosperity** are Human-Centered Design and Potential for Impact. Two fixes would help most:
  - a measured awareness result;
  - evidence that the people reached influence hiring.

## 3. Economic Prosperity, criterion by criterion

### Alignment: 3 of 5
- **Solve's wording:** "The solution uses technology to address one of Solve's Global Challenges." The focus area is "Quality employment pathways, especially for young people and excluded workers".
- **In the draft:**
  - women as the excluded workers;
  - the 77% of the Nifty 50's gap that is about employment;
  - "From awareness to jobs".
- **The gap:** the pathway reads as awareness first and jobs later, while reviewers look for how a pathway opens.
- **The fix:**
  - Name the employment decisions that awareness reaches: questions on hiring raised at AGMs, fund stewardship letters, and managers' hiring calls.
  - Keep the 77% figure in the first paragraph, because it shows the gap is about jobs.

### Potential for Impact: 2.5 of 5
- **Solve's wording:** "The planned solution implementation has the potential to impact the intended population."
- **In the draft:**
  - NSE's 13 crore investors;
  - the Saudi and 60-country findings;
  - Denmark's disclosure law;
  - the awareness measure.
- **The gap:**
  - There is no result yet.
  - The intended population benefits through other people's decisions.
  - The closest Indian evidence on light-touch interventions is negative. The draft rightly says so.
- **The fix:**
  - Report the awareness pilot in numbers before 23 October.
  - Set targets for reach and for the size of the awareness change.
  - Add one sentence on how many employment decisions the reached stakeholders control, for example how many companies' shareholders are reached.

### Feasibility: 3.5 of 5
- **Solve's wording:** "The team has a realistic, practical plan for implementing the solution, and it is feasible in the given context."
- **In the draft:** live products, a dated plan with tickets, and the measurement design.
- **The gap:**
  - no team section and no budget;
  - the markets still in development;
  - legal checks marked "to confirm with counsel".
- **The fix:**
  - team names and roles;
  - a twelve-month plan with milestones and a budget;
  - a line on who gives legal advice, and when.

### Innovative Approach: 3.5 of 5
- **Solve's wording:** "The solution includes a new technology, a new application of technology, a new business model, or a new process for solving the Challenge."
- **In the draft:**
  - the payroll you own;
  - the predict-the-room game;
  - markets that resolve on the next refresh;
  - the prior art, and how the solution differs from each.
- **The gap and the fix:** see [solve-innovation-assessment.md](solve-innovation-assessment.md). A measured result is what moves this to 4.

### Human-Centered Design: 2 of 5
- **Solve's wording:** "The solution is designed with and for underserved communities, and the solution team demonstrates that they have the experience, relationships, data, and knowledge to develop and deliver a successful solution."
- **In the draft:**
  - women seeking work and fair pay as the people the work benefits;
  - The Equal Lens's work on bias through play;
  - the game's player-tested design.
- **The gap:** under the team's framing, the users are savers, investors and the stakeholders around hiring, and none of them is underserved.
- **The fix, within that framing:**
  - Show the design was tested with the people it reaches: the 14-player night, the sixteen-player stress test, the interviews and the awareness pilot.
  - Show The Equal Lens's experience and relationships, with numbers from its programmes.
  - Say plainly that women gain through the decisions of the people Nivel reaches.

### Scalability: 3 of 5 (finalists only)
- **Solve's wording:** "The solution has a plan for financial viability and the potential to be scaled to affect the lives of more people."
- **What is strong:**
  - a static site, with near-zero cost per reader;
  - five disclosure regimes already covered;
  - a feed that keeps the game fresh;
  - anytime play, which needs no host.
- **The gap:** grants are the only funding, and none is named.
- **The fix:**
  - Name the grants and prizes being pursued.
  - Give a yearly running cost and a cost per person reached.
  - Present the planned data sets, US boards and fund footprints, as the scaling path.

### Partnership Potential: 3.5 of 5 (finalists only)
- **Solve's wording:** "The applicant clearly explains how the solution would benefit from the broad range of resources that the Solve community is positioned to provide."
- **In the draft:**
  - funding for the awareness pilot;
  - introductions to fund houses and stewardship groups;
  - behavioural economists;
  - partners in other disclosure countries.
- **The fix:** make each ask specific. Name the kinds of Solve members (asset managers, foundations working on women's employment) and what each introduction would unlock.

### Technical Feasibility: 3.5 of 5 (finalists only)
- **Solve's wording:** "If the underlying technology is novel, the applicant has provided convincing evidence that it has been built and functions as they claim it does."
- **What is strong:**
  - Nivel is public: 675 companies, 3,401 shareable pages, every figure traced to a filing, and no accessibility violations.
  - Spot the Stereotype has run a real night.
- **The gap:** the markets are not built.
- **The fix:** show a working, clickable forecasting question in the demo video (planned for week 3), and describe the markets as in development until then.

## 4. The Learning challenge
- **Its question:** "How can we close global education gaps while improving outcomes for every learner?"
- **Its focus areas:**
  - "Strengthen teachers' instructional practice through evidence-grounded tools"
  - "Work in emergency or low-resource settings"
  - "Include assistive technology or multilingual capabilities"
  - "Improve assessment of what learners truly know and can do"
- **Who it is for:** learners everywhere, with emphasis on:
  - children out of school;
  - learners with disabilities;
  - speakers of non-dominant languages;
  - communities in low-resource or emergency settings;
  - public education systems.
- **What it says about adults:** nothing on adult learning, workforce skills, social-emotional learning or bias education.
- **Its prizes:**
  - the $10,000 Solver award;
  - up to $150,000 for AI for Humanity;
  - $100,000 for Citizens Workforce Innovation, which gives priority to workforce problems in Boston and New York and so does not suit Nivel;
  - up to $200,000 for Community Catalyst;
  - up to $100,000 for E Ink;
  - $150,000 for Seeding the Future.
- **Nivel's fit is weak.**
  - The only hook is assessment: Nivel would measure what people believe about others and about the gap, before and after.
  - Its learners are adults with money in the market, not students, so reviewers would see a stretch on Alignment and Human-Centered Design.
- **What would fit: The Equal Lens's school programmes for ages 5 to 18** (Discovery, Teen Compass and Relay).
  - Their play-based sessions led by teachers and facilitators speak to the first focus area.
  - They would need a technology component, their own application, and The Equal Lens's child-safety rules.
  - That is a separate decision for the team.

## 5. What to change next, within the team's framing
1. **Report the awareness pilot in numbers** before 23 October. This raises Potential for Impact, Innovation and Human-Centered Design at once.
2. **Name the employment decisions that awareness reaches,** and how many companies or funds the reached stakeholders influence.
3. **Add the team, a twelve-month plan and a budget.**
4. **Add a funding plan:** the grants and prizes being pursued, the yearly cost, and the cost per person reached.
5. **Show a working forecasting question in the demo,** or call the markets "in development" throughout.
