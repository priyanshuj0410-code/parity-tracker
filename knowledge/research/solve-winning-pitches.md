# What wins at MIT Solve

Six winning applications were read from their own Solve solution pages on 2 October 2026. They span every stage, from concept to scale.

- **Use of quotes:** the quotes below are as extracted from those pages. Check each one against the page before reusing it.
- **A caution:** the 2026 winners came from the 10th Anniversary Global Challenge, which took only solutions "at scale" or "scale-ready", with unit economics and a track record. They show what a mature application looks like. Nivel applies at Pilot stage, so ThriveLink, which won at pilot stage in 2024, is the closest model.

## 1. In one minute
- **Every winner names one excluded group,** sizes it in the first lines, and serves those people directly: they use the product themselves.
- **Every winner proves an outcome in money, time or lives,** even from a small pilot. ThriveLink won with 28 patients and $20,859 in benefits secured.
- **The AI does a specific job a scarce person cannot.** It enrols a family by phone in 2 to 5 minutes, screens an eye on an offline phone, or turns a CV into transferable skills. It is never decoration.
- **Each pitch carries the same supporting points:**
  - a cost comparison or unit economics;
  - a policy window that makes this the moment;
  - a team close to the people it serves;
  - a partner who brings distribution;
  - a payer who is not the beneficiary.
- **Nivel's draft is strong on rigour and originality and weak on all of these,** above all because the people it helps are not its users.

## 3. What counts as a winning pitch
1. **A named, excluded group, sized in the first lines.**
   - Karya: 2 billion informal workers.
   - Making Space: 61 million disabled Americans, twice as likely to be unemployed.
   - ThriveLink: 130 million US adults who cannot reach safety-net programmes.
   - The reader knows who the pitch is for before knowing what the product is.
2. **A mechanism a reader remembers.**
   - "Taking a job can mean losing the services that keep someone alive."
   - "Poor people can't access resources because they can't complete the paperwork."
   - One sentence explains why the problem persists.
3. **The people helped use it themselves.** Workers take Karya's tasks, parents get Rocket Learning's activities and families call ThriveLink. Nobody's benefit depends on a third party acting first.
4. **Outcomes in money, time or lives, at any stage.**
   - Scale-stage winners report income: 25.8% for Karya, $37,200 for Making Space.
   - The pilot winner reports one small, concrete pilot: 28 patients, $745 each.
   - Reach alone is never the proof.
5. **AI with a specific job.**
   - Each AI does something a scarce professional cannot do at that price or reach: an ophthalmologist, a social worker, a benefits adviser.
   - The AI for Humanity Prize covers "data science, artificial intelligence, and/or machine learning", so real data science qualifies too.
6. **A cost comparison or unit economics:**
   - under $2 against $5 to $8 per screening;
   - 2 to 5 minutes against 20 to 60;
   - $0.10 per child a year.
7. **A policy window.**
   - Making Space cites the ABLE Age Adjustment Act, which widens eligibility to 6 million more people.
   - Rocket Learning cites India's added $150 million for Anganwadis.
   - ThriveLink notes that hospitals are now required to screen for these needs.
8. **Proximity.** Teams built from or with the community: ThriveLink's team and its co-design with patients, Onvkuce from inside the Nation, and Making Space's disabled founder. This is what Solve's Human-Centered Design criterion scores.
9. **A distribution partner, and a payer who is not the beneficiary:**
   - hospitals pay for ThriveLink and Visilant;
   - AI companies pay for Karya's work;
   - employers partner with Making Space;
   - government channels carry Rocket Learning.
   - The person helped pays nothing.
10. **One clear sentence:** the technology, the user and the outcome. For example: "a smartphone-based AI platform enabling community health workers to conduct clinical-grade eye screening".

## 4. What each stage must show
- **Concept:** only through the Indigenous Communities Fellowship in 2027. Onvkuce won as a concept that came from inside the community.
- **Pilot (Nivel):** one pilot with a measured outcome, a clear user, and a credible next step. ThriveLink is the model: one clinic, 28 people, one outcome in dollars.
- **Growth:** consistent use and outcomes, as with Visilant's 50,000 screenings and Making Space's 10,000 users.
- **Scale:** millions reached, unit economics, and government or market channels, as with Rocket Learning and Karya.

## 5. The AI for Humanity Prize
- **What it is for:** solutions "leveraging data science, artificial intelligence, and/or machine learning to benefit humanity". The Patrick J. McGovern Foundation funds it.
- **Winners:**
  - 2024: Consultation Corral, SXD, Just Value, ThriveLink and Hunu.
  - 2025: EdLight, Gyre Energy, Onvkuce: Voice Database, Vinco and Visilant.
  - 2026: Karya and Rocket Learning.
- **What its winners share:** the AI or data work is the reason the solution reaches people a human service cannot, and the application says exactly what the model does.
- **For Nivel, honestly:**
  - The product uses no AI today.
  - Its data work, which turns five disclosure regimes into one comparable scale, is real data science and is what the prize's wording allows.
  - Claim that, and only claim AI that is built and working by the deadline.

## 6. Nivel's draft against the pattern
| What winners do | Nivel's draft now | To close the gap |
|---|---|---|
| name one excluded group, sized first | the pitch opens with owners. The people served now come first under "Who it serves" (40.0% of women in work or looking; 27.7% in towns) but not in the opening | open with women kept out of paid work and the misperceived norm that keeps them there |
| the people helped use it themselves | savers and young professionals use it; the women it is for meet it second-hand | a family-facing version and a partner working with women returning to work |
| outcomes in money, time or lives | reach facts only: 675 companies, one night of 14 players | the Guess first test and one measured workplace night before 23 October, reported with numbers |
| AI with a specific job | none | claim data science honestly; AI only if built |
| unit economics | not stated | the cost of serving a reader (a static site, near zero) and of a night (a host's time) |
| a policy window | present but unframed | BRSR Core's wage-share disclosure, the EU's CSRD, and India's 2025 law ending real-money opinion trading |
| proximity | The Equal Lens's play programmes, not yet described | describe The Equal Lens's existing work with schools, families and communities, with numbers, and the partner |
| a distribution partner and a payer | grants; paid data services later | employers paying for workplace nights would follow the Making Space pattern. Name one fund house or employer pilot |
| one clear sentence | two sentences covering three products | one sentence: technology, user, outcome |

**A one-sentence draft:** "Nivel turns companies' mandatory pay disclosures into a free picture of the payroll a saver owns, and uses a predict-the-room game and play-money forecasts to correct the beliefs that keep Indian women out of paid work."

## 7. What would most improve the application
1. **Open with the people served and the misperceived norm,** not the owners. Keep the payroll footprint as the hook for owners and employers.
2. **Turn the planned pilot into a ThriveLink-style result:** one setting, a small number of people, and one measured change, reported plainly.
3. **Name a payer:** employers buying workplace nights, which keeps Nivel and anytime play free. This is a team decision.
4. **Name one partner with reach to the women served,** and one employer or fund house for a pilot.
5. **Write the one-sentence solution,** and frame the policy window (BRSR Core, CSRD, the 2025 Act).
6. **For the AI prizes,** claim data science, and claim AI only where it is real.

## 8. Decisions taken since
On 2 October 2026 the team set the framing:
- **The problem and the users:** unawareness of bias is the problem, and savers, investors and the stakeholders who influence employment decisions are the users.
- **The outcome:** awareness, measured before and after.
- **The organisation:** The Equal Lens is a nonprofit, with no payer and no partner for this work.

**How the winners' pattern applies now:**
- **A payer:** this does not apply to a grant-funded NGO. Report the cost per person reached instead, as Rocket Learning did with $0.10 per child a year.
- **A partner:** this does not apply either. The proximity point is answered by testing the design with savers, investors and players.
- **A measured pilot result:** still essential. It becomes ThriveLink's shape with awareness in place of dollars: one setting, a small group, and the change in awareness before and after, reported plainly.
