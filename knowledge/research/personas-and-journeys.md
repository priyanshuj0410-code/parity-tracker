# Nivel personas and customer journeys

The shareable version of this page is at https://claude.ai/artifact/H1DLwZgkbTFQPhgRv7mN2H (private until shared from its Share menu).

This document covers who Nivel, Spot the Stereotype and Nivel's markets are for, and how a person moves between them.

- **These are proto-personas:** assumptions written down so they can be tested.
- **What they draw on:**
  - published data;
  - what the products already know: one game night of 14 players, no interviews yet, and no analytics on Nivel;
  - the plans in [spot-the-stereotype-anytime-and-nivel.md](../plans/spot-the-stereotype-anytime-and-nivel.md) and [mit-solve-application.md](../plans/mit-solve-application.md).
- **Each persona ends with what to ask in interviews.** Section 6 is the plan to test the personas before the Solve demo on 23 October.

## 1. Who the experience is for
| Part | Its job | Primary personas |
|---|---|---|
| See it: Nivel's data | show owners how the payroll they own is split between women and men | Ananya, Kavita |
| Play it: Spot the Stereotype, as hosted nights and anytime play | correct what people assume about others, among friends and against the crowd | Rohan, Farah |
| Forecast it: markets in Nivel | let people back a belief and see it resolve against data and surveys | Arjun |

**The people the work is ultimately for** are women kept out of paid work, and the people around them whose beliefs decide it (Meera and Rajesh below).
- They are the reason for the work, yet today the experience reaches them only second-hand.
- Solve's Human-Centered Design criterion asks for a solution "designed with and for underserved communities", so this gap matters. Section 5 covers it.

## 2. Personas

### P1. Ananya, the index-fund saver
- **Role:** primary, See it.
- **Who:** 29, Bengaluru, product analyst. She puts a monthly SIP into a Nifty 50 index fund through a broker app, and follows two personal-finance creators.
- **In her words:** "I picked an index fund so I wouldn't have to think about it."
- **Wants:**
  - her money to match her values without it becoming a second job;
  - a number she can understand in ten seconds;
  - something worth sharing.
- **Believes now:** an index fund is neutral, and the gender gap means unequal pay for the same job.
- **What would change her mind:**
  - The payroll she owns, in rupees. ₹10 lakh in a Nifty 50 fund owns ₹26,968 of payroll a year, and ₹5,604 of it goes to women.
  - The split: 77% of the gap is too few women employed, not lower pay per woman.
- **Meets us through:**
  - a creator's post or a share card on Women's Day;
  - a news story that quotes Nivel;
  - Guess first on an index page.
- **Worries:** being lectured, greenwashing, and that acting means selling her fund.
- **Grounded in:**
  - NSE's unique registered investors passed 13 crore in April 2026, and one in four is a woman.
  - Nivel's Nifty 50 figures for 2025-26.
- **Ask in interviews:**
  - Do you know what your index fund owns?
  - What would you do with this number?
  - Who would you tell?

### P2. Rohan, the young professional who plays
- **Role:** primary, Play it.
- **Who:** 26, Pune, software engineer. He plays word games daily, and found the one company workshop on gender he attended preachy.
- **In his words:** "Just don't make it a lecture."
- **Wants:** a quick, clever game he can win by being sharp, and something to argue about with friends.
- **Believes now:** most people his age hold more traditional views than he does, and women who stop working chose to.
- **What would change his mind:**
  - predicting the crowd and finding it more equal than he assumed;
  - learning that 44.4% of women outside the labour force give child care or running the home as the main reason.
- **Meets us through:**
  - a friend's invitation to a night;
  - an anytime set shared in a group chat;
  - a workplace night run by his employer.
- **Worries:** being judged for an answer, and data about him being kept.
- **Grounded in:**
  - The game's content is written for urban Indians aged 20 to 35, and one night has run with 14 players.
  - Tiltfactor found that games which do not announce their message work better.
  - Urban women's labour force participation was 27.7% in 2025, against 45.9% in villages (PLFS 2025).
- **Ask in interviews:**
  - What did you think other players would say?
  - What surprised you?
  - Would you play alone, and when?

### P3. Farah, the host
- **Role:** primary, Play it.
- **Who:** 33, Delhi. She runs community events and volunteers with The Equal Lens. In the workplace version of this persona, she is an HR learning lead running a night for her team.
- **In her words:** "I need it to run itself while I watch the room."
- **Wants:**
  - a night she can run from one console;
  - a theme her group cares about: Bollywood for friends, the workplace for colleagues;
  - proof it was worth everyone's time.
- **Believes now:** data kills a room, and a workplace theme has to feel local.
- **What would change her mind:** a workplace round where the room guesses the Nifty 50 figure and then argues about the 77% split.
- **Meets us through:** The Equal Lens, or "Run a night" on Nivel's `/play` page.
- **Worries:**
  - the tech failing on a call;
  - someone sharing something personal;
  - what is kept about her players.
- **Grounded in:**
  - the game's run sheet and its review of sixteen players in four teams;
  - rooms go dark after 12 hours;
  - one night has run.
- **Ask in interviews:**
  - What made the last night work?
  - What would you need to run a workplace night next month?

### P4. Arjun, the forecaster
- **Role:** primary, Forecast it.
- **Who:** 24, Hyderabad, analyst at a start-up. He used an opinion-trading app until real-money opinion trading shut in India in 2025.
- **In his words:** "I like being right before everyone else."
- **Wants:** questions with a clear answer and a date, a record of how he did, and a legal place to forecast.
- **Believes now:** women's work is a topic, not a cause, and he holds no view on it.
- **What would change his mind:** forecasting women's labour force participation, then being wrong in a way he can see.
- **Meets us through:** a market linked from a Nivel figure, a friend's link, or search.
- **Worries:** that play money is pointless, and that questions are loaded.
- **Grounded in:**
  - Probo and MPL Opinio shut their real-money operations after the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025.
  - Play-money markets forecast about as well as real-money ones (Servan-Schreiber and others, 2004).
- **Ask in interviews:**
  - What would make a play-money market worth your time?
  - Did resolving against a survey feel fair?

### P5. Kavita, the stewardship analyst or journalist
- **Role:** secondary, See it.
- **Who:** 38, Mumbai, ESG analyst at an asset manager. In the media version of this persona, she is a business journalist.
- **In her words:** "I need a number I can defend in front of a committee."
- **Wants:** comparable, sourced figures, downloads, and the working shown.
- **Believes now:** BRSR data is too messy to compare, and rankings across countries mislead.
- **What would change her mind:** every figure traced to its filing, with the gaps labelled.
- **Meets us through:** a search for one company's women's wage share, or a colleague's link.
- **Worries:** anything that looks like an ESG rating under SEBI's rules, and errors.
- **Grounded in:**
  - BRSR Core asks for gross wages paid to women as a share of total wages.
  - The EU's SFDR makes funds report the gender pay gap of the companies they hold (principal adverse impact indicator 12).
  - The CFA Institute's 2023 report on 300 Indian companies' BRSR shows the profession's interest.
- **Ask in interviews:**
  - Which figure would you put in a voting rationale?
  - What would stop you citing Nivel?

### P6. Meera, weighing a return to work
- **Role:** the person the work is for.
- **Who:** 31, Indore, commerce graduate. She left her job after her first child and is thinking about going back.
- **In her words:** "I don't know who would back me if I went back."
- **Wants:** an employer that hires and keeps women, and support at home.
- **Believes now:** most people in her family's circle expect her to stay home.
- **What would change her mind:**
  - seeing that more people around her support women working than she thought;
  - seeing which large employers actually employ women.
- **Meets us through:**
  - today, second-hand: a figure a relative brings home;
  - later, a Hindi share card or a partner organisation.
- **Worries:** being judged, childcare, and her standing in the family.
- **Grounded in:**
  - 44.4% of women outside the labour force in 2025 named child care or running the home as the main reason (PLFS 2025).
  - In less gender-equal countries, people underestimate how many others support women working (Bursztyn and others, 60 countries).
- **Ask in interviews** (through a partner, in Hindi):
  - Who decides whether you work?
  - What do you think your neighbours think?

### P7. Rajesh, the family decision-maker
- **Role:** the household around the person the work is for.
- **Who:** 52, Lucknow, runs a small business. He has a say in whether his daughter-in-law takes a job.
- **In his words:** "People talk."
- **Wants:** his family respected, and financial security.
- **Believes now:** most men he knows would look down on a working daughter-in-law.
- **What would change his mind:** credible evidence that most men like him privately support it. That is the Saudi finding.
- **Meets us through:** nothing directly today. Possible routes are his children, a WhatsApp forward of one figure, or a family theme in the game.
- **Worries:** status, safety, and who looks after the home.
- **Grounded in:**
  - Bursztyn, González and Yanagizawa-Drott (2020).
  - A national test of correcting Indian men's beliefs is under way, with results forthcoming (Bertrand, Dizon-Ross and Schofield).
  - In Karnataka, neither a video nor a guided conversation moved families' support for women working (Dean and Jayachandran).
- **Ask in interviews** (led by a partner): How do you think other men in your community feel about women working?

## 4. Moments that matter
1. **The guess before the reveal.** Committing to a number first makes the correction stick. This is Guess first in Nivel and round 1 in the game.
2. **The split.** "77% of the gap is who gets hired" turns a story about unequal pay into one about who gets jobs at all.
3. **The crowd is kinder than you thought.** This is the misperceived-norm correction, played rather than lectured.
4. **The price moves after new information.** Changing your mind is visible, and nobody is punished for it.
5. **The figure you take home.** A share card, and the conversation at the dinner table.

## 5. Gaps the journeys expose
- **The people the work is for are reached only second-hand** (Meera and Rajesh).
  - Needed: Hindi, cards that travel on WhatsApp, a family theme in the game, and a partner organisation working with families.
  - This is the main risk on Solve's Human-Centered Design criterion.
  - **Decision, 2 October 2026:** the work targets savers, investors and the stakeholders who influence hiring. Women are its beneficiaries, not its users, and no partner is planned. The family-facing ideas above are out of scope for now.
- **Nivel counts nothing.** There are no analytics, so there is no reach figure. Either decide on a cookieless aggregate counter, or say plainly that there is none.
- **Earning tokens for markets** needs a design that does not depend on accounts in the game, which has none.
- **The "ask your fund" step does not exist.** It is the action an owner can take.
- **Players arriving from the game** need a sector view in Nivel.
- **A workplace night needs a counts-only export** before a manager sees it (`sharing.md` section 4).
- **Interface references (Lazyweb):**
  - Yahoo Finance and Coinbase now place prediction markets beside financial data and explain crowd-set prices on the page. That is the pattern Nivel's markets follow.
  - BuzzFeed's quiz pages show how a light quiz leads into more of the same.

## 6. Validating the personas before 23 October
- **Interviews of 30 minutes:**
  - 5 savers;
  - 5 players, including some of the 14 from the first night;
  - 3 hosts;
  - 3 forecasters;
  - 5 managers or HR leads, the stakeholders closest to hiring decisions.
  - The scripts are the "Ask in interviews" lines above.
- **A Guess first test with 20 people on the Nifty 50 figure.** Record the guesses as counts. The guesses are also the first evidence of the misperception the pitch claims.
- **One workplace night with before-and-after questions** on what players think their peers believe.
- **Afterwards, mark each persona as held, changed or cut,** and update the journeys. Until then, nothing here is a finding.
