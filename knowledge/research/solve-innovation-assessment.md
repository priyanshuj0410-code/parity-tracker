# MIT Solve innovation assessment

The shareable version of this page is at https://claude.ai/artifact/Xw7xheBKz9RyiGs1e4AyXm (private until shared from its Share menu).

This assesses the pitch in [mit-solve-application.md](../plans/mit-solve-application.md) as it stood on 2 October 2026.

- **Method:** desk research on that date. The literature findings were checked against the paper or its publisher's summary, not a search snippet. Section 6 gives the limits of the search for past Solvers.
- **The scale is ours.** Solve publishes no scoring scale, so the scores here are an internal 1 to 5.

## 1. The verdict
| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | 3 of 5 | Every part has prior art. New: a free, public footprint in rupees of the payroll a holder owns, built on BRSR Core's women's wage share and split into employment and pay; and markets that resolve on a disclosure dataset's own refresh |
| Originality | 4 of 5 | No product was found that joins an owner's view of the payroll they hold, a game that scores reading the room rather than opinions, and forecasting on the same figures. It joins climate finance and behavioural economics, two fields that rarely meet |
| Credibility | 2.5 of 5 | The data is strong and traceable. There is no evidence yet that the experience changes beliefs, the closest Indian evidence on light-touch interventions is negative, and the markets are not built. 3.5 is reachable by 30 October |
| Innovative Approach, as Solve defines it | 3.5 of 5 | It fits "a new application of technology" and "a new process". 4 is reachable with the changes in section 9 |

**Innovation is not the weakest criterion.** Human-Centered Design and Potential for Impact are (section 8).

## 2. What Solve scores
The criteria below were read from the 2027 challenge page on 2 October 2026.
- **Innovative Approach:** "The solution includes a new technology, a new application of technology, a new business model, or a new process for solving the Challenge."
- **Also scored for every application:** Alignment, Potential for Impact, Feasibility and Human-Centered Design.
  - Human-Centered Design reads: "The solution is designed with and for underserved communities, and the solution team demonstrates that they have the experience, relationships, data, and knowledge to develop and deliver a successful solution."
- **Scored for finalists only:** Scalability, Partnership Potential and Technical Feasibility.
- **The focus area to enter:** "Quality employment pathways, especially for young people and excluded workers". The draft application calls it "Quality Employment and Economic Opportunity" and should use the page's wording.
- **No scoring scale is published.**

## 3. What the pitch claims is new
1. **Climate finance's attribution method, applied to pay:** if you own 1% of a company, you own 1% of its payroll and 1% of its gap.
2. **One comparable scale across five disclosure regimes,** with every step shown.
3. **Play and forecasting that resolve against data and surveys,** used to correct misperceived norms.

## 4. Direct competitors: gender data for owners
| Who | What it does | Overlap | Where Nivel differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equileap | a Gender Equality Scorecard of 19 criteria for thousands of listed companies, sold to investors and used in indices with Solactive, Morningstar, Euronext and Indxx | gender data on companies, for investors | Nivel is free and public, does arithmetic on disclosures rather than scoring, and shows the rupees a holder owns. Equileap covers policies and leadership that Nivel does not |
| As You Sow, Gender Equality Funds | a free tool that grades thousands of US funds on Equileap's metrics | the closest match to Nivel's planned fund footprints (PARITY-34) | US funds and letter grades. Nivel gives a currency footprint, starts with India, and splits employment from pay |
| EU SFDR, principal adverse impact indicator 12 | makes funds report the average unadjusted gender pay gap of the companies they hold, weighted by market value | ownership-weighted attribution already exists, in regulation | SFDR averages percentage gaps. Nivel weights by the payroll owned, so it reads as money, and separates employment from pay. SFDR figures sit in fund documents few savers open |
| LSEG (formerly Refinitiv) | data and research on portfolio gender pay gaps and investment strategies | portfolio pay-gap analytics | paid and institutional |
| CFA Institute and CFA Society India, Mind the Gender Gap (from 2023) | reports on women's participation and pay in Indian listed companies, the second covering 300 companies' BRSR disclosures | BRSR gender data for India | reports, not a live, comparable, multi-year tool |
| Factly | data journalism on BRSR, such as 147 women to every 1,000 men in key managerial roles | public stories from BRSR | articles, not a tool |
| Gender Pay Gap Bot (UK) | quote-posts employers' pay gaps under their Women's Day posts; it went viral in 2022 | visibility from disclosed data, at the right moment | aimed at employers' messaging, not owners. Nivel can borrow its timing |
| The disclosures themselves: BRSR, the UK service, WGEA, Japan's database, CSRD statements | publish the raw figures | the data | five formats and measures. Nivel's work is making them comparable |

## 5. Indirect competitors and analogues
| Who | What it does | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Tiltfactor's Buffalo and Awkward Moment | party card games tested in experiments | games that hide their message reduce bias more, which supports "points for prediction, never for opinion" |
| Half the Sky Movement mobile games (2012), including Family Values for India | narrative games on the value of girls and their education | gender games have run in India before, evaluated mostly on how they were received |
| Breakthrough's Taaron ki Toli | a classroom programme over two and a half years | sustained programmes do shift attitudes (section 7) |
| Gapminder's misconception questions, and Ipsos's Perils of Perception | quizzes showing that people misjudge facts | guess-then-reveal is established and engaging |
| Family Feud (a game show since 1976), and the Bayesian Truth Serum (Prelec, 2004) | ask people to predict what others answered | predicting the room is an old, familiar mechanic. Using it to correct gender norms is the new part |
| Manifold (play money), Metaculus and Good Judgment Open | forecasting platforms and tournaments | play-money forecasting works, and few questions there are about gender norms |
| Polymarket and Kalshi; Yahoo Finance and Coinbase showing market odds beside financial data | real-money markets moving into finance apps | the interface pattern for markets next to data (Lazyweb captures) |
| Probo and MPL Opinio (India) | real-money opinion trading, shut after the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 | a legal gap that earned play money can fill, and a regulatory line to stay well inside |
| Myna Mahila Foundation's RANI (a 2025 Solve applicant; its page shows no Solver status) | AI-matched work for low-income urban women | what Solve sees in this focus area: direct routes into jobs |
| Karya (a 2026 Solver team) | paid digital work for rural Indians | Solve funds Indian teams that put earnings directly in underserved hands |

## 6. Past Solver teams: what wins
- **2026, the 10th Anniversary Global Challenge:**
  - Ten Solver teams were chosen from over 1,000 applications in 110 countries, each with at least $100,000.
  - Two are Indian: Karya and Rocket Learning, both winners of the AI for Humanity Prize.
  - Making Space won the Citizens Workforce Innovation Prize.
- **2025:**
  - The Citizens Workforce Innovation Prize went to ELEVATE and Tatum T1.
  - The Andan Prize for Refugee Inclusion went to Hello World, Nabu and NaTakallam.
  - No 2025 Solver team found works on gender norms or on investor disclosure.
- **2017:** Solve ran a Women and Technology Challenge on women's full economic participation, so the theme has had a home before.
- **The pattern:** Solver teams serve a named, underserved population directly, show traction (users, pilots and partners), and use technology to deliver at scale.
- **No past Solver found joins investor disclosure with norm correction.** That leaves room, and no precedent to point to.
- **Limits:** the searches read press releases and two solution pages. Check Solve's solutions directory by focus area before quoting any of this in the application.

## 7. The literature

**What supports the proposal:**
| Finding | Study |
|---|---|
| Most young married men in Saudi Arabia privately supported women working outside the home but underestimated how many other men agreed. Correcting the belief raised sign-ups for a job-matching service for their wives | Bursztyn, González and Yanagizawa-Drott (2020), American Economic Review |
| In 60 countries covering 80% of the world's population, misperceptions of gender norms are pervasive. In less gender-equal countries, people underestimate support for women working outside the home, particularly support among men | Bursztyn, Cappelen, Tungodden, Voena and Yanagizawa-Drott, NBER Working Paper 31049 |
| Across 434 measured perceptions, misperceptions about others are widespread. Correcting them usually shifts beliefs, and sometimes behaviour, often only right after | Bursztyn and Yang (2022), Annual Review of Economics |
| Social norms are a barrier to women's employment in developing countries | Jayachandran (2021), IMF Economic Review |
| In India in 2025, 40.0% of women aged 15 and over were working or looking for work, and 27.7% in towns. 44.4% of women outside the labour force named child care or running the home as the main reason | Periodic Labour Force Survey, Annual Report 2025 |
| A sustained programme in 314 Haryana schools (a 45-minute session every three weeks for two and a half years) improved gender attitudes by about 0.2 standard deviations, and boys' behaviour with them | Dhar, Jain and Jayachandran (2022), American Economic Review |
| Pay-gap transparency narrowed Denmark's gap by 2 percentage points (13%), mainly by slowing men's wage growth, and the firms it covered hired more women | Bennedsen, Simintzi, Tsoutsoura and Wolfenzon (2022), Journal of Finance |
| A simple rating moved money: after Morningstar's sustainability globes appeared, funds rated low lost more than $12 billion and funds rated high gained more than $24 billion, with investors using the simple rating and ignoring the detail | Hartzmark and Sussman (2019), Journal of Finance |
| Play-money markets forecast as well as real-money ones | Servan-Schreiber, Wolfers, Pennock and Galebach (2004), Electronic Markets |
| Prediction markets aggregate what many people believe into a price | Wolfers and Zitzewitz (2004), Journal of Economic Perspectives |
| Games that obscure their message reduced bias more than ones that announce it | Kaufman and Flanagan, Tiltfactor |

**What cautions against it:**
| Finding | Study |
|---|---|
| In Karnataka, neither a testimonial video nor a guided conversation raised families' support for women's work. Shifting norms "might require more sustained, community-level action" | Dean and Jayachandran, J-PAL |
| Of 418 prejudice-reduction experiments from 2007 to 2019, 76% test light-touch interventions whose long-term effects are unclear, and there are signs of publication bias | Paluck, Porat, Clark and Green (2021), Annual Review of Psychology |
| India's national test of correcting married men's beliefs, with 25,000 couples, has no results yet | Bertrand, Dizon-Ross and Schofield, J-PAL |
| Attitudes moved in Haryana while aspirations changed far less and household chores barely shifted | Dhar, Jain and Jayachandran (2022) |
| Investors chase a simple rating, which is also why SEBI regulates ESG ratings: Nivel has to stay arithmetic on disclosures, not a rating | Hartzmark and Sussman (2019); SEBI's ESG rating provider rules |

## 8. Assessment

### Novelty: 3 of 5
- **New:**
  - A free, public footprint in rupees of the payroll a holder owns, built on BRSR Core's women's wage share, with the gap split into employment and pay. No public tool found does this in India.
  - Markets whose questions resolve on a disclosure dataset's own refresh.
- **Not new:**
  - ownership-weighted gender metrics (SFDR indicator 12, As You Sow, LSEG);
  - company gender scores (Equileap);
  - guess-then-reveal quizzes (Gapminder, Ipsos);
  - predict-what-others-said mechanics (Family Feud, the Bayesian Truth Serum);
  - play-money forecasting (Manifold and others).
- **Reviewers will know the prior art.** Anyone from impact finance knows SFDR and Equileap, so the pitch must name the difference or look unaware of them.

### Originality: 4 of 5
- **No product found joins the three parts:**
  - an owner's view of the payroll they hold;
  - a game that scores reading the room rather than opinions;
  - forecasting on the same figures.
- **It joins two fields that rarely meet:** attribution from climate finance, and misperceived norms from behavioural economics.
- **The counts-only privacy design is unusual for a social game,** and fits The Equal Lens's work with young people.
- **Why not 5:** each piece exists somewhere, and the joins are not built yet (the feed, anytime play, the markets).

### Credibility: 2.5 of 5 now, 3.5 reachable by 30 October
- **Strong:**
  - Nivel is live:
    - 675 companies, seven indexes and five disclosure regimes;
    - every figure traced to its filing, with gaps labelled;
    - no violations in its accessibility audit.
  - The game works, and its content is governed:
    - the owner reviews every entry;
    - sources are read as raw text;
    - safety framing is built in.
  - The problem and the mechanism rest on strong research (section 7).
- **Weak:**
  - No outcome evidence yet: one night, 14 players, and no before-and-after questions.
  - The closest Indian evidence on light-touch interventions is negative, and the national test is not in.
  - The markets are not built.
  - Reach is unmeasured, because Nivel counts nothing.
  - The people the work is for are not yet users (personas P6 and P7 in [personas-and-journeys.md](personas-and-journeys.md)).
  - Two legal checks are open: SEBI's ESG rating rules for the data, and the 2025 Act for the markets.
  - The team's details are not yet in the draft.

### Innovative Approach, as Solve defines it: 3.5 of 5
- **It qualifies twice:**
  - as "a new application of technology": disclosure arithmetic and markets applied to women's employment;
  - as "a new process": see, play and forecast on one dataset.
- **It is neither a new technology nor a new business model.**
- **4 is reachable** with the changes in section 9. A 5 would need evidence that the process changes beliefs.

### Where the application is weaker than on innovation
- **Human-Centered Design.**
  - The problem: the primary users are savers and urban professionals, not the underserved women the challenge names.
  - The fix: name women excluded from paid work and their families as the people served, with a partner organisation, interviews and Hindi.
- **Potential for Impact.**
  - The problem: the chain from a saver's screen to a woman's job is long.
  - The fix: name the two short links, owners asking companies and families' beliefs, with a measure for each.
- **Alignment.**
  - The problem: the focus area is employment pathways, but the pitch mostly describes informing people.
  - The fix: say how the work opens pathways, through employers hiring women and families allowing work.

## 9. Changes that would raise the score before 2 November
1. **Name the prior art,** with the difference in one line each: Equileap, As You Sow, SFDR indicator 12, Gapminder and Manifold.
2. **Lead with what is new:**
   - the payroll you own in rupees, split into employment and pay;
   - a game that scores reading the room;
   - markets that resolve on the next refresh.
3. **Collect first evidence before the demo on 23 October:**
   - Guess first with 20 people on the Nifty 50 figure;
   - one workplace night with before-and-after questions on what players believe their peers think.
   - Report it as a test of the process, not of impact.
4. **Meet the light-touch evidence head-on:**
   - nights are repeated community contact;
   - anytime play and markets are repeated exposure;
   - the evaluation (PARITY-45) has a comparison group;
   - claims stay at stated beliefs.
5. **Bring the people served into the work:**
   - a partner organisation working with women and families;
   - five interviews in Hindi;
   - a card made for families.
6. **One line each on the law:**
   - play money, earned and never bought, with no prizes, under the 2025 Act;
   - arithmetic on disclosures, not a rating, under SEBI's rules.
7. **Claim AI only where it is real,** for the AI prizes.
8. **Use the challenge page's exact wording** for the focus area.
9. **Decide on reach numbers:** either a cookieless aggregate counter for Nivel, or say plainly that there are none.
