# ISSB as Nivel's global anchor for gender data

The owner asked on 13 October 2026 to look at the ISSB as the global standard for the cross-market dataset ([../plans/cross-market-dataset.md](../plans/cross-market-dataset.md), decisions 6 and 7). The crosswalk ([gender-disclosure-crosswalk.md](gender-disclosure-crosswalk.md)) already catalogues its instruments; this note says what that means for Nivel.

## What the ISSB asks for on gender today

- **IFRS S1 (general requirements, June 2023):** no gender metric. It requires material sustainability-related information and tells the entity to consider the SASB Standards in identifying it.
- **IFRS S2:** climate only.
- **SASB Standards (maintained by the ISSB, amended December 2023 for international use):** an Employee Diversity and Inclusion topic in a few industries only (software and IT services, professional and commercial services, asset management, investment banking among them). The metrics are the percentage of gender and diversity-group representation by level: management, technical or professional staff, all other employees. No pay or pay gap metric.
- **Taxonomies:** the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Taxonomy covers S1 and S2; a separate SASB Standards Taxonomy tags the SASB metrics. Neither gives Nivel's three measures (share of pay, share of staff, share in each level) as elements.

## Where the ISSB's workforce work stands

- **Project:** "Workforce-related Disclosures", renamed from "Human Capital" on 24 September 2026 (12 of 12 members agreed). Research stage: no decision to start standard-setting.
- **Evidence so far:** the December 2025 research findings saw clear evidence that investors need better disclosure of an entity's workforce strategy, risks and opportunities.
- **Scope under study:** workforce size, composition, compensation, engagement, turnover, training, working conditions, health and safety, in the own workforce and the value chain. The project page does not name gender or pay equity.
- **Now:** in October 2026 the staff took the jurisdictional landscape for workforce disclosures to the Sustainability Standards Advisory Forum, asking how social, cultural and regulatory differences affect a global baseline. That is the question Nivel's crosswalk answers for six markets.

## Adoption across Nivel's markets

| Market | ISSB-based standard | Gender content |
|---|---|---|
| UK | UK SRS S1 and S2, published 25 February 2026; FCA PS26/19 (30 September 2026) requires listed companies to report against them | None beyond S1; gender comes from the pay gap regulations and the Listing Rules |
| Australia | AASB S2 mandatory for Chapter 2M reporters; AASB S1 voluntary | None; gender comes from WGEA |
| Japan | SSBJ standards, issued 5 March 2025, phased in for large TSE Prime companies | None; gender comes from the Cabinet Office Ordinance and the WAA |
| India | Not adopted. ICMAI issued its own ISS1 and ISS2 on 29 January 2026; SEBI's BRSR is its own format | BRSR's Essential Indicators are the richest gender set Nivel has |
| EU | ESRS, with an ISSB interoperability guide (May 2024) that covers climate only | ESRS S1: headcount, pay gap, top-management share |
| US | None federal | EEO-1 is not public |

## How Nivel should use it

1. **As the frame, not the source.** The ISSB is where a global workforce standard would come from, and it is being adopted market by market. Today it supplies no gender figures, so Nivel's data stays with the regimes that do: BRSR, the UK pay gap regulations, WGEA, the Japanese ordinance, ESRS S1.
2. **Map the ids to it where it has metrics.** The crosswalk's representation-by-level data points already cite SASB codes (for example TC-SI-330a.3); keep that mapping, and use GRI 405 and ESRS S1 for pay definitions until the ISSB has its own.
3. **Watch the workforce project, and consider taking part.** If it moves to standard-setting, its composition and compensation metrics would become the global baseline Nivel's ids should follow. The research is asking now how jurisdictions differ; the crosswalk (324 data points across six markets) is direct evidence for that question, and The Equal Lens could offer it through the ISSB's outreach.
