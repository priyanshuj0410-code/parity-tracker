# Parity 50, methodology version 2

## Why a version 2
- **Version 1** ([0007](0007-parity-50.md)) held the Nifty 50's sector mix within 2 points and exactly 50 companies. That made it hold companies where women get as little as 1.5% of the pay:
  - It kept the best of each low-parity sector, such as Bosch at 8.1%.
  - It used companies with small payrolls, such as Adani Power at 1.5%, to fill places. A small payroll does not move the payroll-weighted figure.
- **The owner's decision (3 October 2026):** a parity index must not hold such companies at any weight. Every holding must clear a bar, set at the market's own level.
- **What was seen before this was written:** on 2026 data only, how many companies clear the bar and the parity figures of a portfolio built this way. No return of this version had been computed.

## Universe and dates
- **Universe:** Nifty 500 members as on 30 September 2026. Survivorship bias is reported with the results.
- **Rebuilds:** the last trading day of September. Backtest portfolios: 2024 and 2025. 2026 is the current composition.

## Data
- **Point in time,** as in decisions 0006 and 0007:
  - market capitalisation from NSE's file on the date;
  - free float from the latest shareholding pattern on or before 30 June;
  - the company's own BRSR for the fiscal year that ended that March, received by NSE by the date. Its women's share of wages must be strictly between 0 and 1, its women's share of permanent staff must be reported, and its payroll must be reported, not estimated.

## The bar
- **The bar** is the Nifty 50's own women's share of the payroll it owns, at free-float weights, from the same data that year. It was 21.5% in 2024, 20.5% in 2025 and 20.8% in 2026.

## Eligibility
A company is eligible when it:
- has at least 1,000 permanent staff, so that a handful of employees cannot set its figure; and
- pays women at least the bar as a share of its total wages.

## Selection and weights
- **Holdings:** the 50 largest eligible companies by free-float market capitalisation. If fewer than 50 qualify, all of them. Counts checked before any return: 57 in 2024, 70 in 2025 and 79 in 2026.
- **Weights:**
  - each company's free-float market capitalisation times its women's share of wages divided by the bar;
  - each company capped at 8%, with the excess shared out in proportion until no company is above the cap;
  - no sector limits.

## Returns and comparison
- **Total return:** month-end NSE closes. Splits and bonus issues are adjusted, dividends are reinvested, demerged shares are valued until they list, rights issues are adjusted, and renamed companies are followed by ISIN, as in [0007](0007-parity-50.md#correction-to-the-return-calculation-2-october-2026-after-the-first-run). Weights drift between rebuilds.
- **Comparison:**
  - the Nifty 50 at free-float weights, from the same data;
  - for context, the SBI Nifty 50 ETF and the ICICI Prudential BSE Sensex ETF.

## Reported
- **Returns:** return, volatility and largest fall; tracking error and beta against the Nifty 50.
- **Parity:**
  - the bar each year;
  - women's share of the payroll owned;
  - women's share of pay and of staff at the average holding (weighted by holding), against the Nifty 50's;
  - the lowest holding's women's share of pay.
- **What the bar keeps out:** the Nifty 50 members left out, with their weight and the reason, and the Nifty 50 sectors with no holding.
- **Sensitivity checks:**
  - free-float weights without the tilt;
  - a bar of 15% instead of the market's level.

## Limits stated with the results
- **History:** two years.
- **Survivorship:** Nifty 500 membership of September 2026.
- **Concentration:** there are no sector limits, so whole sectors are absent and financial services and IT carry much of the weight.
- **Data:** a company's wages and its staff figures can cover different workforces.
- **Not advice:** past and simulated results do not predict future returns. The index is fictional and shown on Nivel as a simulation.
