# Thoth and Nivel: gender-norm markets

## The idea
- **Nivel** shows what is true: women's share of pay, staff and board seats, company by company.
- **Thoth** is a play-money prediction app for India. Its tokens (Feathers) are earned, never bought, and its markets are already live.
- Joining them turns facts people scroll past into beliefs people test. There are three loops:
  1. **Forecast the data.** Markets on Nivel's own next figures and on public statistics. Examples: "Will the Nifty 50's women's share of pay rise in FY2027?" (resolves on Nivel's next refresh), and "Will urban women's labour force participation in the next quarterly bulletin be above X%?" (resolves on the government's Periodic Labour Force Survey).
  2. **Forecast the norm.** Markets on what other people believe, resolved against published surveys, such as "What share of Indian men say a woman should be allowed to work outside the home?"
     - This targets misperceived social norms. Bursztyn, González and Yanagizawa-Drott (2020) found that most Saudi men privately supported women working outside the home but underestimated how many other men agreed, and that correcting the belief changed behaviour.
  3. **See your own bias.** After resolution, each player sees how their forecasts on women's outcomes compared with the data, as calibration by topic.

## Resolution sources (to confirm schedules)
- **Nivel's own yearly refresh:** pay share, staff share and board seats.
- **Periodic Labour Force Survey quarterly bulletins:** women's labour force participation.
- **Time Use Survey:** unpaid care work.
- **National Family Health Survey:** attitudes and decision-making questions.
- **World Values Survey and Pew attitude questions:** norm questions.
- A market resolves only against a named, dated publication, and the source is shown before anyone forecasts.

## Safeguards
- **Adults only.** The Equal Lens works with under-18s, so Thoth markets stay 18+, and no programme for children or teens uses markets or tokens. Equal Lens child-safety rules apply to any shared content.
- **Neutral wording:** questions about norms describe beliefs without endorsing them, and every result links to the source.
- **No individuals:** markets name companies or populations, never people.

## Legal
- **Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025:** play money only. Tokens are earned, cannot be bought or cashed out, and carry no prizes of monetary value. Confirm the Nivel-linked markets keep this unchanged.
- **Data:** no personal data beyond what Thoth already holds.

## Evaluation
- A short pre and post question on each norm a player forecast: what do they think others believe?
- With a single-group pre/post design, the claim is limited to stated beliefs, not behaviour.
- A comparison group (players who never saw the result) would strengthen it.

## MIT Solve
- **Best fit:** the **2027 Global Economic Prosperity Challenge**, focus area "Quality Employment & Economic Opportunity".
  - It asks how to "expand access to quality work, affordable housing, and financial resilience". Its brief names the gender gap directly: "women remain 24% less likely than men to participate in the labor force".
  - Misperceived norms about women working outside the home are exactly what the Thoth loop targets, and Nivel makes pay and employment gaps visible to the people who own the companies.
  - The 2027 Global Learning Challenge (teachers, assessment, low-resource classrooms) is a weaker fit.
- **Deadline:** 2 November 2026, 12:00pm EST. Applications opened on 22 September 2026.
- **Stage and type:** concept to scale, for-profit or nonprofit, any country. Both apps are live, so apply as **Pilot**.
- **Funding:**
  - Every selected team gets a $10,000 Solver award.
  - Prizes that may fit: the AI for Humanity Prize (up to $150,000), the Crescent Enterprises AI Prize (up to $100,000) and the E Ink Innovation Prize ($100,000).
  - The US-based prizes don't apply.
- **The pitch:** visibility (Nivel) plus belief correction (Thoth), built by The Equal Lens.
- **Inputs:** live products, the audit, coverage figures and the evaluation design.
